`ANSI/TIA-942` rates a facility across four domains — electrical, mechanical, telecommunications and architectural — and defines the spaces that carry it. At this scale the standard does not ask for a raised-floor data hall. It asks for the same spaces, instantiated in one locked cabinet.

| Locked cabinet · 12U | |
| --- | --- |
| Service demarcation and uplink | `MDA` |
| Switch — premises network and cross-connect | `HDA` |
| AI node — accelerators, storage, telemetry | `EDA` |
| UPS — ride-through and orderly shutdown | Electrical · support |

## Telecommunications

— demarcation, uplink and horizontal cross-connect, all in-cabinet. Structured cabling reaches the rest of the premises; single-pair cabling reaches the sensors and actuators beyond it.

## Electrical

— one UPS on the building supply. About 1 kW of IT load, an ordinary circuit rather than a new service.

## Mechanical

— about half a ton of cooling, inside the envelope the closet already maintains.

## Architectural

— roughly five square feet. The cabinet locks, which is what gives a compliance officer an answer about physical access.

> The reduced build is what the standard permits at this size, not a shortcut around it. The rated facility domains are the same ones the resident node monitors.
